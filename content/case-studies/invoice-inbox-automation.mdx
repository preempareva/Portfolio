## The situation

Finance operations received invoice emails from 20+ companies, spread across 24 mailboxes.

## The problem

The inputs were inconsistent: different formats, senders and attachment styles. Sorting and filing them was heavy manual work.

## Why it mattered

Invoices that sit unsorted slow down everything that comes after them, and manual sorting doesn't scale with more companies.

## Constraints

- Inconsistent inputs from many senders.
- Microsoft 365 tooling the team already used.
- The finance team had to own and run it after I left.

## Investigation and discovery

My first design was a single central flow that handled every mailbox. It didn't handle the inconsistent inputs reliably: one sender's quirk could break processing for everyone.

## Approach

I redesigned it as independent per-company flows. Each one handles its own sender's format, and a problem in one doesn't stop the rest.

## Implementation

- Power Automate flows, one per company.
- SharePoint for storage.
- Structured Outlook folders so the team can see where everything went.
- Testing and migration before handover.

## Tools and systems

Power Automate, SharePoint, Outlook.

## Outcome

A stable, team-owned setup covering 24 mailboxes, handed over after testing and migration.

## What I learned

When inputs are messy, isolating failures beats one clever central flow. The first design failing taught me more than it cost.

## What I would improve next

A shared monitoring view across all flows, so the team can spot a failing flow at a glance instead of checking each one.
