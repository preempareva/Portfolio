## The situation

The Imports team sent a logistics status report every day. Each coordinator needed the slice for the brands they handled.

## The problem

Building it took 3–4 hours of manual work daily: filtering large Excel files, mapping coordinators across 60+ brands and emailing people one by one. Doing that by hand every day carried a high risk of errors.

## Why it mattered

Hours of skilled time went into copy-and-paste every day, and a mistake meant someone acting on the wrong numbers.

## Constraints

- Source data arrived as large spreadsheets and SAP extracts.
- Every coordinator needed their own report, and only theirs.
- The report had to land reliably every day without anyone babysitting it.

## Investigation and discovery

I mapped the manual routine step by step: which filters were applied, how coordinators were matched to brands, and which cases counted as exceptions, such as clearance delays and missing transit data.

## Approach

Turn the routine into a pipeline: process the data once, apply the same exception rules every time, split by coordinator, and send.

## Implementation

A Python dispatcher that:

- processes the source data,
- applies the exception rules and builds exception sheets,
- maps each brand to the right coordinator,
- creates brand-wise, coordinator-specific reports, and
- emails them automatically.

## Tools and systems

Python, pandas, openpyxl, SAP API, SMTP.

## Outcome

- 3–4 hours of daily manual work replaced by automated dispatch.
- Zero manual intervention and consistent daily reports.
- Tested and production-ready on 16 April 2026.

## What I learned

Write the manual routine down exactly before automating it. Most of the logic was already there in people's heads.

## What I would improve next

Add a simple run log and an alert when a source file is missing or late, so a failure gets noticed before anyone asks for the report.
