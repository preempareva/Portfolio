## The situation

This is a grouped case study. It covers the pattern behind the investigations I've written up at Media.net, not one single project.

## The problem

A funnel metric drops, two reports disagree, or an experiment result looks off. The movement alone never explains what happened.

## Why it mattered

Teams need a cause they can act on and defend, not a guess.

## Constraints

- Not every source is documented, so nothing can be taken on trust, including table names and old notes.
- Business numbers stay confidential, so this page describes the method rather than the results.

## Investigation and discovery

The same checks come up every time:

- **Is the data fresh?** A table can look right and still be months out of date.
- **Is the grain what I think it is?** One row per what?
- **Can the join duplicate or drop rows?**
- **Do the definitions match** between the two things being compared?

## Approach

1. Check the data is real.
2. Segment it.
3. Make several guesses and test each against the data.
4. Compare the relevant periods or cohorts.
5. Write it up.

## Implementation

Each write-up records the finding, the likely cause, the impact, the current status and the next step, so anyone can pick it up later.

## Tools and systems

SQL, Tableau, product analytics.

## Outcome

Every investigation ends as a write-up with the likely cause, impact, status and next step, giving teams a clearer basis for action. The running count is in the snapshot above.

## What I learned

How to turn a vague "something looks off" into a root cause, and that checking the data first saves the most time later.

## What I would improve next

Turn the most common checks into reusable queries, so every investigation starts from the same baseline.
