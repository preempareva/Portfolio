## The situation

Traffic quality checks flag sessions that look like bots. For one campaign, the raw "bad traffic" rate looked alarming.

## The problem

Nobody knew whether that flagged traffic was actually costing money, or only looked bad on paper.

## Why it mattered

If it was a revenue leak, it needed fixing quickly. If it wasn't, a scary number could push the team toward the wrong action.

## Constraints

- Bot-check results and click records live in different places and had to be linked reliably.
- Campaign, partner and revenue details stay confidential, so this write-up is deliberately generic.

## Investigation and discovery

I joined bot-check results to click records and checked the match quality first: about 99.7% of records matched, high enough to trust the analysis built on top.

Segmenting the suspicious activity showed that crawler re-hits were inflating the "bad" share.

## Approach

Validate the join, then segment, then separate what only inflates a rate from what actually costs money.

## Implementation

- Linked the two sources and measured the match rate.
- Segmented the flagged activity.
- Traced the inflated share to crawler re-hits.

## Tools and systems

SQL, Redshift, data quality checks, traffic analysis.

## Outcome

About a 99.7% match rate. Crawler re-hits were inflating the bad share, and there was no revenue leak.

## What I learned

A high "bad" rate is a question, not an answer. Check what the rate is counting before deciding what it means.

## What I would improve next

Report crawler re-hits as their own category so the headline rate doesn't mislead next time.
